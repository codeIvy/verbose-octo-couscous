Keep the engine

Condition without stealing gains.

HIIT / Saturday

Norwegian 4×4

Warm up 8–10 min. Do 4 × 4 min at roughly 90–95% peak heart rate, separated by 3 min easy active recovery. Cool down 5–10 min.

HIIT / Tuesday

Kettlebell swings.

Start with 8 rounds of 20 sec crisp hard-style swings / 40 sec recovery. Build to 10 rounds only while every rep stays explosive.

Zone 2 / Thursday

Easy by design.

30–45 min at a pace where you can speak in sentences. Bike, incline walk, elliptical, or easy rower all work.

Progression

Increase load systematically.

  • Use 1–3 RIR on nearly all working sets; stop before form changes.
  • Reach the top of every rep range at the intended RIR, then make the smallest load jump.
  • Keep exercise choices stable for 8–12 weeks. Log load, reps, RIR, and pain.
  • If a session runs long, cut the final C pair—not compound rest.

When to adjust

Measure. Then change one thing.

  • Keep the starting volume for four weeks if performance and recovery are good.
  • For a true stall, add one set to a relevant early exercise—only one muscle at a time.
  • Two poor exposures in a row: remove 2–4 weekly sets or reduce cardio stress first.
  • A lower-volume week is for accumulating fatigue, not a calendar ritual.

Nutrition

Daily targets.

  • Protein: 1.6–2.2 g per kg body mass per day, across 3–5 meals.
  • Calories: start around 5–10% above maintenance; target 0.25–0.5% body-mass gain per week.
  • Creatine: 3–5 g creatine monohydrate daily. Timing does not matter.

Recovery

Sleep and tracking.

  • Aim for 7–9 hours nightly and a consistent wake time.
  • Track seven-day average morning weight, weekly waist, and monthly photos.
  • Prioritize sleep when deciding whether to add training volume.

The weekly rhythm

Hard work, deliberately spaced.

Six workout days, one full recovery day, and no two-a-days. Swings and 4×4 are dedicated HIIT sessions; Zone 2 stays genuinely easy.
